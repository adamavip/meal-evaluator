Philosophy

Eight principles, one rhythm.

Borrowed from learning science and from the self-paced platforms that work: short lessons, self-standing modules, a diagnostic that tells you what to skip, and mastery proved by a check rather than by seat time.

01

Backward design

Every module states 4 to 6 objectives written as observable behaviours. Every quiz item maps to an objective. Every lesson teaches exactly one, or a tightly linked pair.

02

Bloom's ladder

Lessons move from remember and understand to apply and analyse. The bank keeps the same ladder: foundation, applied, advanced.

03

Microlearning

Three to six minutes. One idea, one animation, one check. Nobody has to find forty minutes to make progress.

04

Scenario first

Each module opens on a realistic CIMMYT situation and returns to it. Maize in Kenya, wheat in South Asia, seed systems in Mexico. No real projects, no real people.

05

Retrieval everywhere

A micro-check after every lesson, a mastery check after every module, a spaced re-check 7 days after passing.

06

Diagnose, then prescribe

The learner is told what to skip as clearly as what to study. The plan is short and every line carries a reason.

07

Mastery, not seat time

A module is mastered when its check is passed, whether or not the lessons were opened. Checks are always available.

08

Mayer's principles

Signalling, segmenting, coherence. Narration as captions with on-screen keywords, learner-paced frames, no decorative motion.

Learner journey

From one diagnostic to one certificate.

1

Diagnostic

One item per topic plus a second in five random topics, shuffled, from the 100-item diagnostic pool. After each answer: Sure or Not sure.

2

Plan

Every topic lands in a band with a reason, a first lesson and an estimate. Three study modules first, the rest folded under Later.

3

Module, lessons, check

Open any lesson or go straight to the five-question check. Pass at 4 of 5 and the module stamps Mastered and leaves the plan.

4

Capstone, certificate

One project from concept note to impact study, eight decisions. All ten mastered plus the capstone unlocks a completion record. No score on it.

Mastery check rules

A fresh draw, every time

Five items from the module's check pool of 13 to 15, covering at least four objectives, never repeating an item seen in this browser until the pool is exhausted. Press draw twice to see the seen items grey out and the next five avoid them.

Lesson anatomy

Five beats, every time.

Widths are proportional to time. The explainer is a storyboard: frames with narration and a scene, played learner-paced with captions and a transcript. A rendered video can take its place later without touching the lesson.

1 Hook20 s · one sentence
2 Explainer90 to 180 s · storyboard, captions
3 Worked example60 to 90 s · text or widget
4 Micro-check30 to 60 s · instant feedback
5 Recap15 s · 3 bullets, §

What every lesson exposes

A transcript, a Read the source link to the framework section, the glossary terms it uses, and a Was this clear? rating stored locally and exportable.

Interactive examples

Seven keyboard-operable widgets: sorter, order, branching scenario, quality dial, MEAL Matrix classifier, budget calculator, persona picker. No drag and drop.

Next for you

The button at the end of a lesson follows the plan, not the module order. It picks the first unfinished lesson of the highest-priority module that still needs study.

Motion language

Eight primitives. No decoration.

Motion does three jobs: explain structure, direct attention, give feedback. If an animation can be removed without losing meaning, it is removed. Timings: 150 to 250 ms for entries, 400 to 800 ms for explanatory moves, one move at a time, no bounces longer than a frame. Everything respects reduced motion.

Scene kinds in the storyboard DSL

title · cards · chain · stack · sort · split · dial · fan · compass · grid · loop · counter · dissolve · checklist. 280 frames across 62 storyboards, about 61 minutes of explainer. The most used: title 63, cards 48, chain 43, split 28, loop 16.

Accessibility and performance

Captions on by default, transcript always visible, no colour-only meaning, no flashing above 3 Hz, no auto-playing loops behind text. Reduced motion shows stills and instant state changes. Interactive pieces never block input.

Architecture

Content in, screens out, nothing on a server.

A Next.js static export. Content is JSON validated by a schema test. The engine is three files of pure functions with a seeded random source for tests. Learner state lives in the browser and exports as JSON.

Content pipeline

Step 1

Author a draft

One bundle per module: module, misconceptions, tags for its ten diagnostic items, new practice and check questions in its own id block.

Step 2

Validate

validate-draft.mjs: ids, beats, scene fields, pools, objective coverage, key balance, option lengths, no em dashes.

Step 3

Merge

merge-drafts.mjs writes modules, appends questions, applies diagnostic tags, collects misconceptions and glossary additions.

Step 4

Test

content.test.ts enforces the schema; engine and learner tests run against the real content with seeded draws.

Step 5

Build

next build exports every module, lesson and check page statically. No server, no database.

Quality and roadmap

Definition of done, by module.

All objectives covered by a lesson and two check items each. Hero explainer with captions and transcript. Check pool of at least 12 items, source-referenced and blind-reviewed. Engine and schema tests green. Playtested by two non-MEAL colleagues who could pass the check after the lessons.
