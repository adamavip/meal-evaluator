## Module 0: Orientation (`orientation`)

### L0.1 How this course works: diagnostic, plan, mastery checks, certificate

- `audio/orientation/l0-1-f1.mp3` (~8s): Start with the diagnostic: fifteen questions, one per topic plus a second in five of them. After each answer, tap Sure or Not sure.
- `audio/orientation/l0-1-f2.mp3` (~14s): Your answers become a plan. Modules land in one of four bands: Start here, Review, Check first or Looking good, each with a reason and the first lesson to open.
- `audio/orientation/l0-1-f3.mp3` (~14s): Each module ends with a five-question mastery check. Four out of five passes. Pass, and the module stamps Mastered and leaves your plan. Miss, and the plan lists the lessons for what you missed and offers a fresh draw.
- `audio/orientation/l0-1-f4.mp3` (~12s): The plan shrinks as checks are passed. When all ten modules are mastered and the capstone is done, you can print a completion certificate. No score on it, only completed.
- `audio/orientation/l0-1-f5.mp3` (~8s): Seven and thirty days after mastering a module, the home page offers a three-question refresher. Everything stays in your browser. Export your learning record whenever you like.

### L0.2 The framework in 90 seconds

- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f1.mp3` (~10s): Why change? Reports can say what was delivered. They cannot show what changed on the ground. The framework builds evidence from observations instead.
- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f2.mp3` (~12s): Evidence is organised along a results chain: processes, outputs, outcomes and impact. A theory of change says how each link is expected to lead to the next, and under which assumptions.
- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f3.mp3` (~12s): Measurement runs through one KPI toolbox: 142 indicators in 13 volumes under three Thrusts. One KPI can be measured at several levels with different valid methods, and institutional totals are recomputed from records, never summed from reports.
- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f4.mp3` (~12s): Data quality has six dimensions. The evidence stack combines direct observations, earth observation, context data and operational records in one data system that collects data once and reports many times.
- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f5.mp3` (~12s): Impact claims are about contribution, not sole cause, and they need a credible counterfactual. Learning runs on a cadence of evidence reviews and decision logs. Accountability runs in four directions.
- `audio/orientation/l0-2-f6.mp3` (~12s): And it all runs through a project lifecycle with gates, a MEAL Matrix that classifies and budgets every project, and a lean MEAL team. By 2030 the question changes from what did we do to what changed, for whom, and what did CIMMYT contribute.

### L0.3 Set your goal

- `audio/orientation/l0-3-f1.mp3` (~10s): Five kinds of people take this course. A project lead needs budgets and requirements. A data officer needs quality rules. A scientist needs the case for observation-level evidence.
- `audio/orientation/l0-3-f2.mp3` (~8s): Pick the one closest to your work. The course keeps every lesson open to everyone and only changes which examples it leads with.

## Module 1: MEAL foundations (`meal-foundations`)

### L1.1 Why MEAL changes: from reports to observations

- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): Two projects report the same yield gain. Only one can show the plots behind the number.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f2.mp3` (~12s): Why now? Major investors ask for evidence of impact as well as delivery outputs. Institutions that can show what changed, how, for whom and why will attract funding, influence policy and sustain partnerships.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f3.mp3` (~22s): A report can confirm that training took place or that farmers were reached. It cannot by itself show whether practices changed, yields improved or benefits persisted. So the new approach works from observation-level records: farmers, plots, trials, households and institutions.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f4.mp3` (~26s): Section 1.1 lists five changes in the role of evidence. From reported figures for compliance to data for learning and impact. From silos to integrated systems. From limited analysis to stronger evidence. From compliance reporting to high-frequency insight. From work that is hard to link to strategy, to credible evidence of contribution.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f5.mp3` (~16s): CIMMYT already produces a large amount of evidence. The task is to connect these sources through common questions, indicators, identifiers and quality rules, so evidence can be reused across projects and aggregated at portfolio and institutional level.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f6.mp3` (~10s): Reporting summaries stay. The objective is to connect them to observation-level data that can be analysed, aggregated and used for learning and impact.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-1-f7.mp3` (~8s): That is the question the new approach is built to answer: what changed, for whom, where, under what conditions and at what scale.

### L1.2 The four strategic effects

- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-2-f2.mp3` (~10s): Evidence: quality data used to understand progress and demonstrate impact. Not figures compiled for compliance.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-2-f3.mp3` (~10s): Collaboration: integrated systems connecting science, MEAL, data, KMIT and management. One shared system instead of five silos.

### L1.3 MEAL in CIMMYT's context: science, partners, geographies

- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-3-f1.mp3` (~10s): CIMMYT is a global agricultural research institution: 50 plus years of maize and wheat improvement, a genebank conserving over 180,000 seed accessions, trial networks in more than 90 countries, and partnerships across the Global South.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-3-f2.mp3` (~12s): This shapes what MEAL means here. Most of the evidence CIMMYT produces concerns scientific contribution to change in agrifood systems, more than service delivery. Its data are a valuable institutional asset, still underused.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-3-f3.mp3` (~16s): The framework connects breeding data from scientists, adoption evidence from socioeconomics teams, agronomic performance data from field programs and outcome data from development projects. Together they form one evidence base.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-3-f4.mp3` (~14s): What becomes possible: credible claims of contribution to yield gains, food security and resilience. Evidence that resonates with investors, governments and scientific peers. Learning that improves breeding and delivery decisions. Accountability to farmers and communities as well as investors.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-3-f5.mp3` (~8s): Scientific contribution, not service delivery. The data already exist. The work is to connect what CIMMYT already measures.

### L1.4 The five evidence functions and when each is used

- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-4-f1.mp3` (~14s): Five functions run across the results pyramid, at every level from processes to impact. They are a practical sequence for designing and operating MEAL in every project: capture, quality assurance, integration, analysis, use and visualization.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-4-f2.mp3` (~12s): Capture. Identify the question first, then capture the observation-level and contextual data needed to answer it. The indicator method decides the source: field measurement, digital records, partner systems, remote sensing or secondary sources.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-4-f3.mp3` (~12s): Quality assurance. Validate, standardise, de-duplicate and document provenance and quality. The controls cover completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness and representativeness.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-4-f4.mp3` (~12s): Integration. Link records across sources, projects, partners, locations and time with common identifiers and controlled vocabularies. Recompute institutional indicators from the underlying eligible records rather than summing project reports.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-4-f5.mp3` (~14s): Analysis applies methods that match the question and the claim, and documents uncertainty and limits. Use and visualization turns evidence into dashboards, maps, briefs and learning reviews for specific users. The purpose is action.

### L1.5 Monitoring, evaluation, accountability, learning: how the four parts depend on each other

- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-5-f1.mp3` (~12s): MEAL is four connected parts. Monitoring keeps routine records of processes and outputs. Evaluation answers whether outcomes occurred and what CIMMYT contributed. Accountability is being open about what works and what does not. Learning turns evidence into adaptation.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-5-f2.mp3` (~14s): Accountability runs in four directions, each with different requirements: to investors and institutional partners, to farmers and communities, to the scientific community and CGIAR, and internally.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-5-f3.mp3` (~12s): Remove one part and the others weaken. Without traceable monitoring records, evaluation cannot link delivery to change. Without evaluation, accountability has nothing honest to say. Without learning, nothing changes next season.
- `audio/meal-foundations/l1-5-f4.mp3` (~8s): By 2030, MEAL will no longer be perceived as a compliance function. It will be a strategic institutional capability.

## Module 2: Theory of change (`theory-of-change`)

### L2.1 What a theory of change does for a proposal

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): Your team knows the variety. The concept note still cannot say how a release becomes a change for farmers.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-1-f2.mp3` (~14s): In Phase 1 the project defines intended results, intervention logic, investor requirements, geography, target populations and feasibility. The theory of change is where the intervention logic gets written down.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-1-f3.mp3` (~14s): Central MEAL co-develops it with you. It traces your activities through outputs and outcomes to impact, so the proposal can say what will change, not only what will be delivered.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-1-f4.mp3` (~12s): The chain feeds the Phase 1 gate: a MEAL section, a KPI crosswalk, an indicative data architecture and a costed MEAL budget, all before submission.

### L2.2 Building the chain: processes, outputs, outcomes, impact

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f1.mp3` (~6s): A results chain is four slabs and three links. The slabs are easy. The links are where proposals go wrong.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f2.mp3` (~14s): Slab one: processes. Trials run with national partners. Slab two: outputs. A variety released, seed sold, field days held. Slab three: outcomes. Farmers plant it. Slab four: impact. Gains that last.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f3.mp3` (~20s): Now hang a tag on each link. Each tag is an assumption: something the chain relies on but the project does not control. Write them down. A link without a tag is a hope, not a plan.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f4.mp3` (~14s): Follow one farmer along the chain. She attends a field day. That is an output. She plants the variety next season. That is an outcome. Nothing at slab two told you slab three would happen.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f5.mp3` (~20s): Now the assumption fails. Certified seed never reaches her agro-dealer. Outputs are complete. The outcome slab stays empty. The chain breaks at the link, not at the slab.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f6.mp3` (~14s): The last link is different again. Impact is lasting change. CIMMYT does not claim to be the sole cause. The chain states a contribution alongside rainfall, prices and other actors.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-2-f7.mp3` (~8s): Slabs are what you deliver and what changes. Links are what you assume. Name each one and plan the evidence that will test it.

### L2.3 Assumptions: the links you cannot control

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-3-f1.mp3` (~6s): Ask one question at every link: what has to be true, outside your control, for the next slab to fill?
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): Seed sold, to farmers plant it: the assumption is that seed reaches the dealers farmers use, at a price they will pay, and that they choose it. Farmers plant it, to lasting gains: the assumption is that the gain persists across seasons and markets.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-3-f3.mp3` (~14s): The Matrix names this check. Seed systems: does the adoption and diffusion logic hold? System change: check the assumptions in the theory of change. Breeding and upstream science: periodic review against the theory of change.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-3-f4.mp3` (~12s): An unstated assumption cannot be checked. A stated one names the evidence that would show it failing. That evidence becomes a row in the measurement plan.

### L2.4 Three pathways compared: seed systems, digital advice, upstream science

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-4-f1.mp3` (~10s): Three project types, three different routes from output to outcome. The Matrix describes each one. Copying the wrong route is a common proposal error.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-4-f2.mp3` (~14s): Seed systems: seed is multiplied and distributed, farmers adopt it, and the variety diffuses. MEAL checks that the adoption and diffusion logic holds, with registries and adoption dashboards.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-4-f3.mp3` (~14s): Digital advice: the chain runs through usefulness and engagement before practice changes. The Matrix asks for a user-centred theory of change covering usefulness, behaviour change and relevance, with usage and engagement KPIs.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-4-f4.mp3` (~14s): Upstream science: the chain connects scientific advances to impact pathways and innovation readiness. It does not pretend that a laboratory output is farmer adoption.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-4-f5.mp3` (~14s): Match the logic to the type. Adoption and diffusion belongs to seed systems. Usefulness and engagement to digital. Innovation readiness to upstream science. Seed logic dropped into a digital project bounces back.

### L2.5 Ownership: who holds which part of the chain

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-5-f1.mp3` (~14s): One RACI row. The project lead is accountable. The Regional MEAL Lead is responsible for doing the work. The Design pillar and the Evaluation pillar are consulted. One A per activity.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-5-f2.mp3` (~14s): Accountable means you own the result, even if someone else drafts it. The Regional MEAL Lead holds the pen. For complex or strategic proposals the Design pillar leads the theory of change work.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-5-f3.mp3` (~12s): The chain is co-developed in Phase 1, confirmed at inception, and revisited in reviews and contribution analysis. Ownership does not end at submission.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-5-f4.mp3` (~8s): For a Small project on the standard package, the Regional MEAL Lead's Phase 1 role is limited to confirming the classification and the base budget. The chain is still yours.

### L2.6 Worked example: draft a chain for a maize variety project in 6 minutes

- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-6-f1.mp3` (~12s): Six minutes, six moves. Four slabs. A tag on each link. Evidence for each tag. Impact as a contribution. One learning question. A pathway check against your Matrix row.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-6-f2.mp3` (~16s): Here is the maize draft. Trials with partners. Variety released, seed sold, field days held. Farmers plant it and companies keep multiplying it. Lasting gains, alongside other factors.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-6-f3.mp3` (~12s): The chain is element one of the minimum package: results architecture. Add the learning questions it raises and the link to CIMMYT Strategy, and it is ready for the MEAL review.
- `audio/theory-of-change/l2-6-f4.mp3` (~8s): Draft it now, at concept-note stage. The Design pillar exists so that weak theories of change are not locked in at proposal stage.

## Module 3: Results framework (`results-framework`)

### L3.1 Four levels, four kinds of evidence

- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): Two projects reported success. Only one measured change.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f2.mp3` (~14s): The framework sorts evidence into four levels. Processes enable delivery. Outputs are what is delivered. Outcomes are what changes for people and systems. Impact is lasting change in agrifood systems.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f3.mp3` (~24s): Watch six results find their level. Trials coordinated: a process. Variety released: an output. Farmers adopting: an outcome. Incomes sustained: impact.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f4.mp3` (~14s): Here is the one everyone gets wrong. Farmers trained tries to land in outcomes. It bounces back. Training is delivered. Adoption is a change.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f5.mp3` (~20s): Even inside outputs, there are two ways to look. Reach counts how many. Quality asks where, to whom, how well and at what scale. Only the second side gives you a foundation for outcome evidence.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-1-f6.mp3` (~12s): Each level has its own data, its own products and its own owner. Projects own processes and outputs. Outcomes are shared. Impact sits with central MEAL and specialists.

### L3.2 Output or outcome? The classification test

- `audio/results-framework/l3-2-f1.mp3` (~8s): Four questions sort any result. Was it done? Was it delivered? Did something change? Did the change last?
- `audio/results-framework/l3-2-f2.mp3` (~14s): Apply the test. A digital advisory tool is deployed: delivered, so an output. Farmers change their planting date after using it: a change, so an outcome.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-2-f3.mp3` (~14s): Partners count too. A seed company multiplying the variety on its own account has changed its behaviour. A ministry adopting a guideline has changed a policy. Both are outcomes.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-2-f4.mp3` (~8s): When in doubt, ask who or what changed. If the answer is nobody yet, you are still at output level.

### L3.3 Reach versus quality: counting people is not measuring change

- `audio/results-framework/l3-3-f1.mp3` (~8s): Reach is a count. Three thousand farmers, twenty events. The number rolls up nicely.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): Quality asks what the count hides. Where were they? Who were they? How well was it delivered? At what scale? Report B answers each question and records an identifier for every participant.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-3-f3.mp3` (~12s): When reach rises and adoption stays flat, nothing is wrong with the data. Delivery is growing. Change has not been shown. Say exactly that.

### L3.4 Policy change as a result

- `audio/results-framework/l3-4-f1.mp3` (~10s): A policy brief is written and published. That is a recommendation produced: an output.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-4-f2.mp3` (~12s): The regulator revises its release procedure using that evidence. A partner's system changed. Policy implementation is an outcome.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-4-f3.mp3` (~12s): Two seasons later, varieties reach farmers a year earlier. Stronger service delivery: still an outcome. Whether yields and incomes rise and stay up is the impact question, and it needs its own evidence.

### L3.5 Crosswalks: one project indicator, several reporting frames

- `audio/results-framework/l3-5-f1.mp3` (~10s): One adoption result. Four places it has to appear: the institutional KPI, the investor template, the CGIAR system and the project's own dashboard.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-5-f2.mp3` (~14s): The crosswalk is a table that says how each one relates to the others. Where definitions differ, it records the difference instead of hiding it. The institutional definition stays as the Toolbox wrote it.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-5-f3.mp3` (~8s): Relabelling is not harmonising. Shared definitions are. If a label is the only thing two indicators share, the crosswalk must say so.

### L3.6 The measurement plan row

- `audio/results-framework/l3-6-f1.mp3` (~8s): Here is an indicator as it often arrives: a name and a target. Nobody can collect it from this.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-6-f2.mp3` (~18s): Section 5.4 lists what the row needs. Unit of observation: plot. Source: crop cuts. Method: net-plot harvest at standard moisture. Frequency: each main season. Coverage: 240 sampled plots in three districts. Disaggregation: gender of plot manager. Owner: the Focal Point. Timing: within four weeks of harvest.
- `audio/results-framework/l3-6-f3.mp3` (~8s): Eight fields. One row per indicator. It is part of the minimum package, so it is finished at inception, not at reporting time.

## Module 4: KPIs & measurement (`kpis-measurement`)

### L4.1 The KPI toolbox: volumes, thrusts and the 142

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-1-f1.mp3` (~10s): The KPI Toolbox holds 142 indicators in 13 volumes. Ten volumes cover impact pathways, which measure CIMMYT's contribution to change. Three cover enabling pathways, which measure how CIMMYT itself performs.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-1-f2.mp3` (~16s): The impact volumes are grouped under three Thrusts: Human Wellbeing, Planetary Health and Peace. That is the top level at which institutional results are aggregated and reported. The enabling volumes sit beside them as institutional performance.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-1-f3.mp3` (~18s): Watch four volumes find their place. Productivity goes to Human Wellbeing. Environmental sustainability to Planetary Health. Policy, finance and markets to Peace. Workforce and talent tries to join Peace and bounces back: it is an enabling volume.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-1-f4.mp3` (~8s): One volume refuses to pick a Thrust. Volume 1, innovation development and scaling, applies across all three, because innovations and partnerships feed every pathway.

### L4.2 One KPI, many valid methods: the yield example

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f1.mp3` (~8s): Three projects, three countries, one yield KPI. Each measures differently. By the end of this story they will reconverge on one reported value.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f2.mp3` (~22s): The yield KPI fans out into three measurement levels. At farm and plot level: direct measurement, crop cuts or a farmer survey. At trial and station level: harvested-plot measurement under standard protocols. At region and country level: aggregation from trials and farm data, with modelling where needed.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f3.mp3` (~18s): Each level has its own key standards. Farm: most reliable method feasible, documented and rated. Trial: net plot, standard moisture, minimum plot size and repetitions. Region: inclusion criteria, weighting, uncertainty bounds.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f4.mp3` (~20s): Take one trial standard. A fresh harvest weighs more than a dry one. So the protocol measures the net plot and adjusts the yield to a standard moisture. The framework's example for maize is 12.5 percent. Only after that dial completes are two trials comparable.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f5.mp3` (~16s): This is what harmonisation means. What is shared: the definition, the methodological logic and the interpretation rules. What varies by level: the method, the key standards and the quality rating.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f6.mp3` (~20s): Now the three levels reconverge. The KPI engine recomputes from the eligible records with the indicator's method. What comes out is one value, and it never travels alone: coverage, uncertainty, provenance and method quality travel with it. The figures here are an illustration.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-2-f7.mp3` (~8s): Projects may set their local scope. They do not silently redefine the institutional indicator. That one sentence is what makes the fan close again.

### L4.3 Aggregation: from plots to institutional totals without double counting

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-3-f1.mp3` (~10s): Three project figures arrive for the annual KPI run. The spreadsheet is begging you to add them, or average them. Both are shortcuts over the records.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-3-f2.mp3` (~18s): The framework goes under the totals. Plot and farm records at the bottom. Trial records above. Inclusion criteria select the eligible records. Then the indicator's method, with weighting by area or representativeness, produces the institutional value.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-3-f3.mp3` (~16s): The method depends on the KPI. Section 3.3 lists what it may involve: de-duplication, weighting, pooling of microdata, extrapolation, spatial interpolation, modelling or meta-analysis, and triangulation with compatible secondary sources.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-3-f4.mp3` (~14s): What comes out is a value with four companions: coverage, uncertainty, provenance and method quality. Then results roll up by KPI volume and by Thrust. Never an unqualified sum of project totals.

### L4.4 Harmonisation and local scope

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-4-f1.mp3` (~14s): Two ways a project can touch an institutional KPI. Local scope says where and for whom it applies. Silent redefinition changes what it means while keeping the label. The first is allowed. The second is not.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-4-f2.mp3` (~18s): Sort four moves. Restricting to irrigated plots: scope. Reporting the main season only: scope. Counting seed recipients as adopters tries to pass as scope and bounces: that is a redefinition. Changing the unit of observation: also a redefinition.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-4-f3.mp3` (~16s): If a team genuinely needs the definition changed, there is a door. Governance rule 1: the MEAL team reviews, the Design, Standards and Enablement pillar approves, and the change is published in a new version of the Handbook. Until then, a project-specific indicator stays project-specific.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-4-f4.mp3` (~8s): Keep the institutional definition. Report the investor measure separately. Write your scope down. Ask for review when the definition itself must change.

### L4.5 Reading a KPI reference sheet

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-5-f1.mp3` (~18s): For every KPI, CIMMYT defines nine things. The concept. The unit of observation. The data sources. The valid methods by measurement level. The calculation and aggregation rules. The disaggregation. The quality assurance requirements. The data provenance.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-5-f2.mp3` (~18s): The methods section reads like a table. One row per measurement level. For yield: farm and plot, trial and station, region and country. Each row names the method, the key standards and the quality rating. Your measurement plan row picks one level and follows that row.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-5-f3.mp3` (~10s): Forms are built from the sheet. Plan rows are checked against it. The KPI engine computes with it. Read the sheet before you plan, and you will not be surprised at the annual run.

### L4.6 Quality ratings and what they tell a reviewer

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-6-f1.mp3` (~14s): A quality rating says how reliable a measurement is, given the method used. Farm-level methods earn high to medium, depending on the method. Trial protocols earn high. An undocumented method earns nothing, because nobody can rate it.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-6-f2.mp3` (~12s): Three levels, three rating bands. Farm and plot: high to medium. Trial and station: high, controlled protocols. Region and country: variable, so document coverage and method quality.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-6-f3.mp3` (~14s): Two records from the same county. Record A carries its method and its rating. Record B carries a number. Only A can be judged eligible, weighted and recalculated later. The rating is what lets a reviewer trust the figure without re-measuring it.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-6-f4.mp3` (~8s): Ratings travel with the record. An aggregate does not inherit the best one in the set. It documents its own coverage and method quality.

### L4.7 From observation-level data to decision-relevant insight

- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-7-f1.mp3` (~8s): The question the framework uses as its own example: what has CIMMYT contributed to yield change in Kenya?
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-7-f2.mp3` (~14s): Three project reports cannot answer it. So the framework dissolves the reports into the observations underneath: plots, trials, farms, participants, each with identifiers and provenance.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-7-f3.mp3` (~16s): Answering needs four things the records make possible. Contribution or attribution analysis against a credible counterfactual. Agronomic, socioeconomic and climate modelling. Adoption data. Geospatial assessment. A sum of reported numbers cannot provide any of them.
- `audio/kpis-measurement/l4-7-f4.mp3` (~14s): From records to decision. Eligible records go into the KPI engine. Out comes a value with coverage, uncertainty, provenance and method quality. It rolls up by volume and Thrust, and leadership gets an answer it can act on and audit.

## Module 5: Data quality & collection (`data-quality`)

### L5.1 Six dimensions of data quality

- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f1.mp3` (~8s): A survey arrives from a seed-systems project in Mexico. Before you report anything from it, you need to know how good it is.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f2.mp3` (~16s): The framework checks six things: completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness, uniqueness and representativeness. This dataset has a problem on every one. The needle sits near the bottom.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f3.mp3` (~14s): Start with uniqueness. Three rows for one farmer become one, because the registry uses a persistent identifier, not a name. The needle moves.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f4.mp3` (~16s): Accuracy and consistency next. Range checks flag the impossible yield and the zero-area plot for investigation at source. Units are standardised with the data dictionary.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f5.mp3` (~14s): Completeness and timeliness. The blanks are followed up, not set to zero. Monthly checks will catch the next batch close to collection, not three months later.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f6.mp3` (~16s): The last segment does not move by cleaning. Only villages near the road were surveyed. You extend coverage, or you narrow the claim to the area you sampled and say so. Then the needle can move.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-1-f7.mp3` (~8s): Six dimensions, one dial. Clean what you can. Document what you cannot. Never report what you have not checked.

### L5.2 Identifiers and provenance: can you find this observation again?

- `audio/data-quality/l5-2-f1.mp3` (~8s): Here is a report figure: 1,240 adopting farmers. Try to get from it back to one farmer, one plot, one form.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-2-f2.mp3` (~18s): Follow one observation forward. A farmer is registered with a persistent identifier. Her adoption answer is recorded against it, with its provenance: form, date, enumerator, method. A later correction is logged in the audit trail. The KPI engine computes the figure from records like hers.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-2-f3.mp3` (~14s): Now try the same walk with a name as the identifier. Three spellings, two farmers with the same name, a phone number that changed. The chain breaks at the first link.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-2-f4.mp3` (~12s): With identifiers and provenance, a figure can be audited, recalculated when the method improves, and reused by the next project. Without them it is just a number.

### L5.3 Outliers, missingness and what to do before deleting anything

- `audio/data-quality/l5-3-f1.mp3` (~10s): Three values from the Mexico survey. A yield far above every neighbour. A plot area of zero. Forty blank adoption answers. Each one tempts a quick fix.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): The framework gives a process, not a threshold. A check flags the value. Someone investigates it at source. A decision follows: correct, exclude with a reason, or keep because it is genuine. Then it is documented in the audit trail.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-3-f3.mp3` (~14s): Watch the two tempting fixes bounce. A blank tries to become a zero and comes back: it is unknown, so follow it up. The extreme yield tries the delete bin and comes back: investigate first.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-3-f4.mp3` (~8s): Investigate before you delete. Record the unknown as unknown. Write down what you did, so the next analyst can see it.

### L5.4 Representativeness: who is not in your sample?

- `audio/data-quality/l5-4-f1.mp3` (~16s): On the left, who was surveyed: two thousand farmers in roadside villages that host demonstration plots, reached in the dry season. On the right, who the claim is about: all smallholder maize farmers in the state. These are not the same population.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-4-f2.mp3` (~14s): Three honest options. Extend coverage to the villages you missed. Narrow the claim to the area you sampled. Or report the wider figure with its coverage, uncertainty and limits stated plainly. Quietly generalising is not on the list.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-4-f3.mp3` (~10s): A big sample of the wrong population is still the wrong population. Size does not fix representativeness. Coverage and honest limits do.

### L5.5 Validation, re-analysis and cadence

- `audio/data-quality/l5-5-f1.mp3` (~14s): Quality runs as a loop, not an event. Data are captured. The form validates at entry. Automated checks raise flags. Exceptions are resolved close to collection. The log records what changed.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-5-f2.mp3` (~12s): This loop runs continuously or monthly. Its outputs are quality flags, corrected records and a system issue log. Nothing waits for the annual report.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-5-f3.mp3` (~16s): The monthly loop sits inside a wider cadence. Quarterly or seasonal reviews turn clean data into decisions. The annual run recomputes KPIs from eligible records and reviews coverage, uncertainty and gaps. Studies come at midterm, endline or when a trigger fires.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-5-f4.mp3` (~8s): Because the records and their provenance are kept, this year's figure can be recalculated next year with a better method. Check monthly. Re-analyse annually.

### L5.6 Roles: who checks what, and when

- `audio/data-quality/l5-6-f1.mp3` (~16s): Quality has four layers. The Focal Point keeps source records and runs first-line checks. The Regional MEAL Lead follows up gaps across projects. The Evidence Systems analyst writes the rules and watches recurrent issues. The Data Group runs the infrastructure.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-6-f2.mp3` (~16s): Watch four tasks find their owner. First-line checks try the Data Group and bounce back to the Focal Point. Follow-up across the region lands with the Regional Lead. The deduplication rule goes to the analyst. The server goes to the Data Group.
- `audio/data-quality/l5-6-f3.mp3` (~8s): Central standards, distributed implementation, institutional integration. Each task has one owner, and the handoffs are explicit.

## Module 6: Evidence & data systems (`evidence-systems`)

### L6.1 The evidence stack: four layers

- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): The MEAL data system does not treat a project report as the primary unit of evidence. It works as close as possible to what is being measured.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f2.mp3` (~12s): Layer one: direct observations. Plot, farm, household, participant, trial and institution records. The closest evidence of the actual state or change, and the only layer you can disaggregate and re-analyse.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f3.mp3` (~12s): Layer two: earth observation and sensors. Satellite imagery, weather, sensors, geospatial layers. They add spatial coverage, frequency and extrapolation between the places you observed.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f4.mp3` (~12s): Layer three: external and context data. Government statistics, public datasets, prices, climate and market series. They supply context, denominators, benchmarks and covariates for interpreting change.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f5.mp3` (~12s): Layer four: operational records. Workplans, training logs, distribution systems, financial and partner records. They give traceability from intervention to output and link who received a service to later uptake.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-1-f6.mp3` (~8s): Four layers, so each result can be checked from more than one angle. But not every indicator needs every source. The KPI reference sheet sets what is valid.

### L6.2 The journey of one observation

- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f1.mp3` (~6s): One crop cut. One record. Follow it through the five evidence functions and watch what it picks up.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f2.mp3` (~18s): Capture. The question comes first: what is the yield on this plot? The form is built from the KPI reference sheet. It records the net-plot weight, the moisture and the plot's persistent identifier. No signal, so it waits on the tablet.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f3.mp3` (~18s): Quality Assurance. On sync the record is checked for completeness, range and duplicates. It gets a quality flag and a provenance entry: who, when, which form version. From now on it can be audited.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f4.mp3` (~16s): Integration. The plot identifier links the crop cut to the farmer's training record, to rainfall for the district and to the satellite field boundary. A shared data dictionary keeps yield meaning yield.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f5.mp3` (~16s): The record now sits in a stack. Each layer adds what the others cannot: coverage from imagery, denominators and context from external data, and from the training log the link between delivery and uptake.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f6.mp3` (~16s): Analysis. At the annual run the KPI engine recomputes the institutional yield from every eligible plot record with the approved method. It reports coverage, uncertainty and method version. Nobody sums the project's number.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-2-f7.mp3` (~16s): Use and Visualization. The same validated record feeds the dashboard, the PRMS submission and two investor exports. When a review decides to act, the decision log points back to this record.

### L6.3 Satellite, context and operational data: what each adds

- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-3-f1.mp3` (~14s): Three layers sit around the direct observation. Earth observation adds coverage, frequency and extrapolation. Context data add denominators and benchmarks. Operational records add traceability from delivery to uptake.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-3-f2.mp3` (~14s): Where the KPI method requires direct measurement, satellite data do not replace it. They calibrate, contextualise, stratify or upscale, under documented rules.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-3-f3.mp3` (~16s): The triangulation rule is a four-part match: construct, population, geography, period. An image from another season tries to count as support and bounces back. Context, yes. Triangulation, no.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-3-f4.mp3` (~8s): Four matches, or it is context. Construct, population, geography, period, under compatible definitions.

### L6.4 Authoritative systems and offline work

- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-4-f1.mp3` (~6s): Section 4 describes the functions the MEAL data system needs, not a specific product. Nine of them.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-4-f2.mp3` (~16s): The first five get the data in and make it trustworthy: multi-channel capture, online and offline; persistent identifiers; quality assurance and provenance; privacy, consent and access; and integration with CIMMYT and external systems.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-4-f3.mp3` (~14s): The last four turn records into results and action: the KPI engine, analytics and geospatial, dashboards and reporting, and learning and feedback that links decisions to the evidence behind them.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-4-f4.mp3` (~14s): Where another system is the authoritative source, breeding, trials, finance or project management, the MEAL data system connects to it. It does not replace it, and it does not copy by hand.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-4-f5.mp3` (~14s): Four design principles: open standards and FAIR, offline where connectivity is poor, modular so each project configures shared components. And one split: the Data Group owns the infrastructure, MEAL owns the content.

### L6.5 Collect once, report many times: investor reporting mappings and the KPI engine

- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-5-f1.mp3` (~6s): One adoption result. Five places it has to appear. The field team collects it once.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-5-f2.mp3` (~16s): The same validated record feeds the institutional KPI, the PRMS submission, both investor templates and the national partner's system. A direct link where the receiving system allows it, an export where it does not.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-5-f3.mp3` (~16s): A new template arrives. The MEAL team maps its fields to source variables already collected, sets up a link or an export, and the project keeps the same forms. A new system means a new mapping, not a new collection.
- `audio/evidence-systems/l6-5-f4.mp3` (~10s): Whatever the destination, the institutional figure comes from the KPI engine: eligible records, the approved method, reported with coverage, uncertainty and method version.

## Module 7: Impact assessment (`impact-assessment`)

### L7.1 Contribution, not attribution

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): Yields rose in the project area. The investor wants to know what CIMMYT did.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-1-f2.mp3` (~16s): Rain was good. Prices were high. A new variety went in through partners. Extension and a county programme ran at the same time. All of these fed one change.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-1-f3.mp3` (~18s): Attribution asks how much of the change the intervention caused, against a counterfactual. Contribution asks what role CIMMYT played alongside the rest. Both are impact methods the framework names. Neither claims sole cause.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-1-f4.mp3` (~10s): CIMMYT does not claim to be the sole cause of observed changes. The aim is credible evidence of contribution, and that is the word to use with the investor.

### L7.2 Counterfactuals: the split-screen farm

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f1.mp3` (~8s): To know what an intervention did, you need to know what would have happened without it. Here is the cleanest way to picture that.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f2.mp3` (~18s): Imagine two identical fields. Same soil, same rain, same prices, same farmer. Only one gets the new variety. At harvest, the gap between them is the effect. Nothing else differed, so nothing else can explain it.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f3.mp3` (~18s): In real life you have one field and two seasons. Yield rose from before to after. But rain and prices rose too. Before-and-after shows change. It cannot show the effect, because the field without the variety is missing.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f4.mp3` (~20s): You cannot run the same field twice. So you build a stand-in: a comparison group of similar farms that did not get the variety, measured the same way over the same seasons. Their result estimates what would have happened anyway. The gap is your effect estimate.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f5.mp3` (~18s): Real farms are noisy. Soils differ, rain is patchy, some farmers were already ahead. The gap is still there, but its edges are blurred. That blur is uncertainty, and the report has to say how wide it is.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f6.mp3` (~14s): The stand-in only works if it is truly comparable. Same thing measured, same kind of farms, same seasons, same method. Break one of these and the gap measures the difference between the groups, not the effect.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-2-f7.mp3` (~12s): Even a clean gap is the effect of the whole package: variety, seed partners, advice. Report it as CIMMYT's contribution alongside other factors, with the uncertainty attached.

### L7.3 Study designs the framework recognises

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-3-f1.mp3` (~14s): Section 2.4 names four method families for impact. Counterfactual impact evaluation estimates an effect against what would have happened anyway. Attribution and contribution analysis ask how much, or what role. Geospatial assessment extends change across space. Modelling and scenario analysis explore what might happen.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): The framework's own example: what has CIMMYT contributed to yield change in Kenya? It needs contribution or attribution analysis against a credible counterfactual, agronomic, socioeconomic and climate modelling, adoption data and geospatial assessment. Several families, one question.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-3-f3.mp3` (~12s): Match the method to the question. How many farmers got seed is descriptive. What did CIMMYT contribute tries to land in descriptive too, and bounces back. A sum of reported numbers cannot answer it.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-3-f4.mp3` (~8s): The Analysis function in one line: apply methods that match the question and the claim, and document uncertainty and limits.

### L7.4 Baselines and timing

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-4-f1.mp3` (~14s): A project starts without a baseline. Two years later the endline measures yields. Good yields. Compared with what? Nobody can say.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-4-f2.mp3` (~18s): Now the same project with the reference decided at inception. Baseline on project and comparison farms before the first season. The same measures repeated each season. Endline with the same method on the same farms. The gap at the end is an effect estimate, with its uncertainty.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-4-f3.mp3` (~14s): Element 6 of the minimum package: a baseline, a comparison group or another valid reference where the method requires it. Otherwise a documented alternative. What it never allows is leaving the question to end-of-project reporting.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-4-f4.mp3` (~8s): Decide the reference at inception. An endline without a reference is a photograph without the before.

### L7.5 Triangulation and direct measurement

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-5-f1.mp3` (~14s): The evidence stack has four layers. Direct observations sit closest to what is being measured. Earth observation, context data and operational records add coverage, interpretation and traceability. Not every indicator needs every layer.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-5-f2.mp3` (~16s): The triangulation rule has five conditions. Same construct. Same population. Same geography. Same period. Compatible definitions. A second source strengthens a claim only when all five hold.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-5-f3.mp3` (~16s): On the left, a project survey from one year and a national series from another, both rising. Looks like confirmation. It is not: different population, different period. On the right, crop cuts and farmer reports from the same plots in the same season. That is triangulation.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-5-f4.mp3` (~14s): And the limit. Where the KPI method requires direct measurement, satellite or secondary data do not replace it. They calibrate, contextualise, stratify or upscale, under documented rules. The ground stays.

### L7.6 Uncertainty: saying how sure you are

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-6-f1.mp3` (~12s): Here is the footprint of one impact estimate. Three of seven districts. Two seasons, both with good rain. Two hundred and forty sampled plots. Before you quote the number, quote this.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-6-f2.mp3` (~16s): Version A: the project raised maize yields in Kenya. Version B: adopters out-yielded comparison farms in three districts over two good-rain seasons, with wide uncertainty, and lasting change not yet shown. Same data. Only one is honest about it.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-6-f3.mp3` (~12s): Section 3.3 names what travels with every institutional result: coverage, uncertainty, provenance and method quality. Never an unqualified sum.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-6-f4.mp3` (~8s): Say how sure you are. The Analysis function requires uncertainty and limits to be documented explicitly, every time.

### L7.7 Shock-event studies and roles

- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-7-f1.mp3` (~14s): The operating cadence has four rows. Continuous checks. Quarterly or seasonal reviews. The annual KPI run. And a fourth row: midterm, endline or trigger-based studies. A drought in season two lights up that row.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-7-f2.mp3` (~12s): Most studies follow the calendar. A trigger-based study follows an event. The framework names shock-event measurement where relevant. Measure while the shock is still measurable.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-7-f3.mp3` (~16s): Who does what. Impact evidence is owned by central MEAL with science and evaluation specialists. The Evaluation, Learning and Accountability pillar is accountable for outcome and impact studies and responsible for contribution analysis. The MEAL Group Lead is accountable for institutional impact claims. The project provides source data, context and implementation history.
- `audio/impact-assessment/l7-7-f4.mp3` (~8s): The project brings the records. The specialists bring the design. Together they turn a shock into evidence.

## Module 8: Learning & adaptation (`learning-adaptation`)

### L8.1 Evidence reviews: the operating cadence

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-1-f1.mp3` (~8s): A dashboard shows a problem in the middle of the season. The question is not whether to react. It is where the reaction gets recorded.
- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-1-f2.mp3` (~16s): The loop has four steps. Evidence enters. A joint review reads it. A decision log entry records what changes, who acts and by when. A practice changes. Then new evidence enters.

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-1-f5.mp3` (~16s): Step four. The practice changes while delivery is still underway. Next quarter the loop runs again and checks whether the change moved uptake. That is adaptive management: during implementation, not only at closure.
- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-1-f6.mp3` (~20s): This loop sits inside a larger cadence. Monthly checks fix records close to collection. Quarterly reviews produce decision logs. The annual run produces the portfolio dashboard, the evidence-gap register and method notes. Studies produce findings when a question needs them.
- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-1-f7.mp3` (~8s): A review without a decision log is a meeting. Evidence in, decision out, practice changed. That is the loop.

### L8.2 Decision logs and management responses

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-2-f2.mp3` (~16s): A decision log entry fills the three fields the framework names. What changes: extend the enrolment window. Who acts: the Focal Point with district leads. By when: end of next month. And the entry is linked to the evidence behind it.

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-2-f4.mp3` (~8s): The data system has a place for both. The learning and feedback functionality holds decision logs, management responses and participant feedback, linked to the evidence behind them. It closes the loop from evidence to action.

### L8.3 When evidence should change a decision

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): The sequence is set by the implementation phase. Investigate anomalies. Use dashboards and reviews to adapt. Document decisions. The gate is a review with actions, owners and follow-up.
- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-3-f3.mp3` (~16s): At the review, evidence takes one of three routes. Clear enough to act: a decision log entry. Not yet clear: a further question with an owner. Beyond routine monitoring, such as why results differ across contexts: a learning review or a trigger-based study with Pillar C.
- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-3-f4.mp3` (~8s): Act on what the evidence supports. Log the rest as a question. Never leave a review with only minutes.

- `audio/learning-adaptation/l8-4-f4.mp3` (~8s): The enabling condition is continuous learning and adaptation: evidence reviewed and acted on, improvements released as new versions of the guidance.

## Module 10: Implementation & governance (`implementation-governance`)

### L10.1 MEAL across the project lifecycle

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-1-f1.mp3` (~6s): MEAL enters a project before indicators, budgets and data systems are fixed. That means the proposal, not the inception workshop.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-1-f2.mp3` (~18s): Six phases, from opportunity and proposal to outcome, impact, synthesis and learning. Engagement intensity scales with the project, but the gates are the same for everyone.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-1-f3.mp3` (~18s): Each phase closes with a gate. Before submission: a MEAL section, a KPI crosswalk, an indicative data architecture and a costed MEAL budget. At inception: an approved MEAL plan and measurement matrix. Before handover: a competency check, a live system and named owners.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-1-f4.mp3` (~16s): The RACI puts names on the work. One accountable role per activity. The project lead is accountable for routine data collection and the Focal Point does it. The Regional MEAL Lead is accountable for the Matrix classification. The Group Lead is accountable for exceptions.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-1-f5.mp3` (~8s): One shortcut. A Small project taking the standard package only needs the Regional MEAL Lead to confirm its classification and its base budget in Phase 1.

### L10.2 The MEAL Matrix: classify your project

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f1.mp3` (~6s): Three weeks to submit. Two choices in the Matrix give you the classification and the budget line.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f2.mp3` (~14s): The Matrix is a grid. Six project types down the side, each with a default complexity. Four scales across the top, set by budget and duration. Every cell holds the support, the KPIs, the deliverables and the base budget.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f3.mp3` (~16s): Start small. A one-year demonstration-plot pilot at USD 150,000 is applied agronomy, so Complex by default, and Small by scale. The base budget is fixed: USD 18,000 for the life of the project.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f4.mp3` (~20s): Now the Kenya proposal: USD 300,000 over two years. Same row, one column to the right: Medium. The rule is 5% of the total budget, which is USD 15,000. But never less than the Small amount for the same complexity. USD 18,000 is the floor, so the counter stays at USD 18,000.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f5.mp3` (~16s): Above USD 800,000 the counter stops. Large and MEGA projects co-design MEAL with the team from the start of proposal development. MEGA adds an institutional contribution of at least USD 27,000 per project year, charged as Core Layer staff time.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f6.mp3` (~16s): Complexity can move. A Small digital advisory pilot starts Intermediate at USD 13,000. It commits to report household-level adoption to the investor. That is a trigger: up one level to Complex, and the counter moves to USD 18,000. The scale has not changed.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-2-f7.mp3` (~12s): Who signs off. The Regional MEAL Lead confirms the classification. Small projects then proceed with the standard package. Medium projects confirm the budget with MEAL while writing. Large and MEGA co-design from the start.

### L10.3 Complexity adjustments and exceptions

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-3-f1.mp3` (~12s): Three commitments move a project up one complexity level: reporting farmer- or household-level adoption, outcomes or impact to investors; running its own ground measurement of outcomes; depending on primary data from several implementing partners.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-3-f2.mp3` (~16s): Watch it work. A digital advisory pilot that promises household-level adoption evidence tries to stay Intermediate. It bounces up to Complex. A discovery project that depends on primary data from four partners moves from Standard to Intermediate.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-3-f3.mp3` (~16s): Three decisions, three owners. The Regional MEAL Lead confirms the classification at proposal review. The MEAL Group Lead is accountable for KPI method exceptions and Handbook changes. The MEAL Review Committee, at the level of scientific leadership, endorses the annual review.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-3-f4.mp3` (~8s): The project lead proposes. The Regional MEAL Lead confirms. The Group Lead decides exceptions. The Committee endorses once a year.

### L10.4 Budgeting the minimum package

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-4-f1.mp3` (~12s): Small projects pay a fixed amount for their lifetime: USD 8,000 Standard, USD 13,000 Intermediate, USD 18,000 Complex. Those three numbers are also the floors for Medium projects.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-4-f2.mp3` (~18s): Medium is a percentage with a floor. Kenya: applied agronomy, Complex, USD 300,000. Five percent is USD 15,000. The floor for Complex is USD 18,000. Take the larger one.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-4-f3.mp3` (~16s): Two more. A USD 500,000 seed systems project: 5% is USD 25,000, above the floor, so USD 25,000. A USD 290,000 breeding project: 4% is USD 11,600, below USD 13,000, so the floor applies.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-4-f4.mp3` (~12s): Above USD 800,000 there is no formula. Large and MEGA budgets are co-designed with MEAL from the start of proposal development. MEGA adds at least USD 27,000 per project year of Core Layer staff time.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-4-f5.mp3` (~16s): What the budget buys. Every project leaves inception with eight elements: results architecture, indicator crosswalk, measurement plan, data architecture, quality plan, baseline or reference, a learning and accountability cycle, and handover and capacity. None waits for the final report.

### L10.5 Project delivery versus institutional MEAL

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-5-f1.mp3` (~8s): The operating principle in six words: central standards, distributed implementation, institutional integration.
- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-5-f2.mp3` (~18s): Row by row. Processes and outputs belong to project teams, with standards and KPI rules from the centre. ### L10.6 Governance, team structure and how the roles work together

- `audio/implementation-governance/l10-6-f1.mp3` (~14s): Three connected layers. The Core Layer sets standards and integrates evidence. The Regional Layer makes it work in each portfolio. - `audio/implementation-governance/l10-6-f2.mp3` (~16s): Inside the Core Layer, three pillars, each with a Senior Lead and two support roles. A: Design, Standards and Enablement. - `audio/implementation-governance/l10-6-f3.mp3` (~14s): Handoffs are explicit. Design defines what and how. Evidence Systems structures, assures, aggregates and visualises. - `audio/implementation-governance/l10-6-f4.mp3` (~8s): Not inside MEAL: software, advanced data science, geospatial processing and corporate data governance. Those come from CIMMYT's technical functions, including the Data Group.
